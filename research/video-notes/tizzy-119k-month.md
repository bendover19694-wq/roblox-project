## 1. Revenue and CCU claim (opening)
- Paraphrased claims:
  - Peak of 38K CCU on a Saturday, with about 25K at recording.
  - About 3.4M Robux earned in 72 h, extrapolated to about $100–119K/month.
  - Rankings were cited from a third-party tracker.
- Calculation check (Inferred):
  - 3.4M Robux per 3 days × 10 = 34M Robux per month.
  - At $0.0035/Robux that is about $119K, which matches the title.
  - The **current** DevEx rate is $0.0038 per earned Robux (Robux held before 2025-09-05 still convert at $0.0035), per the Creator Hub DevEx docs. At $0.0038 the same figure is about $129K. The title therefore uses the old rate.
- Caveats:
  - The 72 h window included the record-peak weekend. The same creator's later post-mortem admits weekend extrapolation overstated another game by about 25–30%.
  - Gross DevEx value excludes team splits, contractor costs, ads, and tax.
  - The game is a "steal" genre game, so it is exposed to the saturation and decay the creator later describes.
- Implication: never plan revenue from peak-weekend run-rates. Plan from D7/D30 cohort ARPDAU × realistic DAU, and model a decay curve.
- Follow-up: the DevEx docs, and a check of the game's current CCU on a tracker.

## 2. Failures before the hit
- Paraphrased claims: a reskin of a trending game, a bought template, a funded team without design sense, and an AI-made trend game all flopped. The about $12K team-funded game peaked at about 1.8K CCU and was unprofitable.
- Implication: reskins, templates, and unpolished AI builds are evidence *against* those approaches, which supports building an original concept.

## 3. "80% idea + execution, 20% optimization"
- Paraphrased claim: Ideas are cheap. Execution (talent, polish, simplicity) is the bottleneck. The remaining 20% is data-driven iteration.
- Caveat: a heuristic, not a measurement.
- Implication:
  - For a solo creator, *execution capacity* is the binding constraint.
  - Pick a concept whose MVP one person can polish, and score "build complexity" and "art burden" heavily.

## 4. Simple mechanic, deep consequences
- Paraphrased claims:
  - Top games pair one easy-to-grasp mechanic with deep replayability.
  - Example: build a base → it gets raided → rebuild with a better layout.
  - Example: one random-roll mechanic in a sandwich game → "what will I roll, how tall can it get."
  - Clear cause and effect: press a button and see the result immediately.
  - "Simple scales, fancy fails."
  - The audience skews young, so complexity loses the mass market.
- Verification of "majority of players are about 9 years old": **likely wrong / Unverified**. Secondary reporting of Roblox's own disclosures puts under-13s at roughly one-third of DAU (Q3 2025), with 18+ at about 26–27% of age-checked users in 2026. The young segment is still large and important, but it is not the majority.
- Implication:
  - Every concept needs **one verb a 9-year-old understands in 5 seconds**, with emergent depth (layout, timing, combination, or social interaction) layered on top.
  - Readability targets young players, but the depth has to retain 13+ players too.

## 5. UX obsession, platform parity
- Paraphrased claims:
  - Copies of hit games fail on polish: broken prompts, unpolished UI.
  - About 30% of *Build a Base and Steal* players were on console. Fixing console onboarding raised D1.
  - Test on console and mobile.
- Implication:
  - Mobile, console, and PC parity is a launch requirement, not a later step.
  - Every slice is tested with a gamepad and on a phone-size screen.

## 6. Retention and virality ingredients
- Paraphrased list:
  - A curiosity hook ("what can I unlock tomorrow").
  - A social flex element: visible status other players see.
  - Strong core fantasy and world (for example, a tiny player in a giant kitchen).
  - **Clippable moments**: study what YouTubers clip. Example: the defender hits a thief who ragdolls into the void.
  - Good monetization.
- Implication: concept scoring includes "clippable moment" and "visible flex" columns. The vertical slice must contain at least one physical-comedy or spectacle moment.

## 7. Iteration volume
- Paraphrased claims:
  - About 9 tutorial iterations shipped on *Build a Base and Steal*.
  - Patches daily, big updates weekly.
  - Adding an intro cutscene on another game cut bounce from about 22% to about 15–16%.
- Caveats:
  - The cutscene result conflicts with rasdev's "no intro" advice, and nothing else changed was controlled for.
  - A daily patch cadence suits a funded team, not a solo creator.
- Implication: test a short, skippable, in-world intro (8 s or less) against no intro, using the first-play bounce segments. Use a solo-sustainable cadence: weekly small, monthly larger.
